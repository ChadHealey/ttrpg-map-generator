# Issue 172 r2 — independent assistant visual review

**Provisional assistant decision: reject this family for visual selection.** I inspected all six
native 1600×800 images and all six 800×400 images in
[comparison-r2](comparison-r2/results.json) against the unchanged
[issue-164 visual contract](../issue-164/visual-contract.md). The comparison with the
[independently reviewed r1 cohort](visual-review-independent-r1.md) uses the same standards.

R2 has real improvements: several coast transitions flow more smoothly, and islands now show
more varied outlines, orientations and a stronger size hierarchy. Parallel sliver groups are much
less prominent. However, broad block-like interiors, flared terminals on narrow stems, and deep
slots still form a repeated, visibly constructed outline vocabulary. Those macro failures remain
clear at half size. No ordinary row receives a provisional visual pass.

This is an **assistant review**, not a human decision. All human visual decisions remain pending;
no production selection or acceptable v3 is inferred. Image inspection does not independently
rerun scalar grids, construction repeats or cross-platform checks.

## Row observations against all six positives

### normal-01 — provisional fail; two visually primary masses

[Native](comparison-r2/normal-01.png) · [Half](comparison-r2/normal-01-half.png)

1. **Primary masses:** the central body and the body continuing across the left/right seam are
   the two primaries. The small left oval is subordinate. The southern pole-containing body
   appears as a wide edge band; projected width alone does not make it an additional primary.
2. **Internal hierarchy:** the two primaries are especially interchangeable in this row: broad
   upper shoulders narrow into a similar lower slot with rounded foot-like terminals. The
   interiors are broad, but the secondary anatomy still reads as a repeated constructed pattern.
3. **Orientation and arrangement:** the polar subordinate and uneven ocean spaces distinguish
   this arrangement from r1. The two primaries nevertheless have similar displayed axes and
   outline relationships. This is primarily an anatomy failure, not proof of a regular global grid.
4. **Water relationships:** broad open ocean is coherent. The two primary lower recesses repeat
   nearly the same sharply bounded notch between swollen terminals.
5. **Margin islands:** unequal small polygonal islands sit near identifiable ends or margins.
   They no longer look like uniformly oriented slivers; there is no decisive R5 finding here.
6. **Half scale:** primary count and broad interiors survive, as do the nearly matching upper
   shoulders and foot/slot anatomy. The main defect is not resolved by reducing edge detail.

**Failures: R2 and R3.** The island improvement does not offset the strong primary-shape repetition.
The southern projection band is not an independent R1 finding.

### connected-majority — provisional fail; six visually primary masses

[Native](comparison-r2/connected-majority.png) · [Half](comparison-r2/connected-majority-half.png)

1. **Primary masses:** six comparably substantial bodies are visible after combining the upper
   and lower seam continuations correctly. Six co-primaries remain interpretable for this explicit
   balanced control; the ordinary one-to-three count rule is not imposed here.
2. **Internal hierarchy:** broad interiors and extensions are present, but almost every body
   repeats a large block, hooked slot and fan/tab terminal. The lower-middle and lower-right bodies
   make this resemblance particularly clear; similar area is not the reason for rejection.
3. **Orientation and arrangement:** axes differ, while comparable body sizes and broad two-band
   spacing reinforce the repeated appearance. Regular arrangement remains a secondary R4 concern,
   not a claim of mathematically equal gaps or channels.
4. **Water relationships:** a visible ocean network separates the bodies. Facing margins repeatedly
   alternate convex blocks and deep slots, so the network lacks varied marginal-water character.
   This visual observation does not prove ocean topology.
5. **Margin islands:** compact groups now contain visibly different sizes and orientations. Similar
   groups still appear beside repeated recesses, but the clear r1 parallel-sliver failure is reduced.
   R5 is a residual concern here, not a carried-over decisive rejection.
6. **Half scale:** the repeated body/slot/tab pattern remains the dominant atlas-wide observation.
   More varied island members do not disguise it.

**Failures: R2 and R3; secondary R4 and R5 concerns.** The full balanced placement is an experimental
achievement, but its repeated internal morphology still fails the unchanged visual contract.

### fragmented-islands — provisional fail; three visually primary masses

[Native](comparison-r2/fragmented-islands.png) · [Half](comparison-r2/fragmented-islands-half.png)

1. **Primary masses:** the upper seam-spanning body, central-lower body and lower-right seam-
   continuing body read as three primaries. The two smaller rounded bodies are subordinate.
2. **Internal hierarchy:** the central primary has a broad interior and a pair of swollen upper
   terminals around a recess. The seam bodies repeat flared appendages and deep cuts. The anatomy
   is less visibly assembled at some joins, but still relies on a small set of recurring shapes.
3. **Orientation and arrangement:** primary axes, ocean openings and subordinate positions differ.
   Additional islands create peripheral fragmentation without resolving the recurring main-body
   structure. Wide upper-edge shapes are assessed with polar projection in mind.
4. **Water relationships:** large open water is coherent. Deep hooked cuts and corner-like
   recesses still determine the primary margins more than distinct broad marginal waters.
5. **Margin islands:** this is a substantial improvement over r1. Large and small irregular members
   vary in direction and form recognizable margin groups. The small curved runs around subordinate
   bodies and repeated primary-side clusters remain concerns, but they are less uniformly mechanical;
   this review does not assert the same decisive R5 failure as r1.
6. **Half scale:** irregular island size hierarchy remains apparent. The primary hooks and swollen
   terminals also remain apparent, so island improvements do not repair the failing macro anatomy.

**Failures: R2 and R3; residual R5 concern.** Preserve the improvement in island variety without using
it as a substitute for the main-body proof.
