## Complete-contract assessment

All seven ordinary rows retain one to three visually primary bodies. The balanced control's
six co-primary bodies are assessed as the declared control, without silently reducing its count.
Broad interiors, secondary lobes, substantial projections and macro recesses are visible across
the cohort. The failing rows fall short in the relationship and variation of those shapes;
this is not a claim that their numeric feature certificates are absent.

Axes, placements, facing directions and opening directions vary. Broad open-water regions remain
coherent and have distinct marginal recesses. I do not find an atlas-wide constant-width channel
pattern. The balanced control has a noticeable two-band composition, but projection alone does
not establish equally spaced spherical centers. Its stronger problem is repeated body and mouth
vocabulary, not the mere presence of equal-area continents. The control images establish neither
implementation of the ocean enum nor production semantic basin connectivity.

Island groups generally have unequal sizes and spacing and follow particular margins. Short
bead-like arcs below the upper companion and around the left companion in `fragmented-islands`
remain localized R5 concerns. They do not establish an atlas-wide necklace failure. No independent
R1 failure is established: these broad bodies and polar bands do not form repeatedly necked
ribbons. There is no separate R6 finding from added noise or overlays; small islands do not
rescue the failed macro relationships.

The broad land, varied water spaces and margin islands survive half-size viewing. So do the
compact repeated cuts in the failed rows. Thus the sixth positive criterion is not satisfied
merely because silhouettes remain recognizable: the required useful macro structure must also
survive without the rejection patterns. R2 is strongest in the balanced control and remains a
cohort concern elsewhere. R3 supplies the decisive failures recorded above; R4/R5 are qualified
concerns, not additional universal failures.
