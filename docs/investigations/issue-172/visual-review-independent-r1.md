## Row observations

### normal-01 — provisional fail; two visually primary masses

[Native](comparison-r1/normal-01.png) · [Half](comparison-r1/normal-01-half.png)

1. **Primary masses:** the central-right body and the body continuing across the left/right seam
   are the two primaries. The upper-left oval and lower-left rounded body read as subordinates.
   Splitting the seam continuation would incorrectly increase the count.
2. **Internal hierarchy:** both primaries have broad interiors and visible extensions, but the
   central body's top block, right paddle and left bent appendage remain discrete construction
   parts. Its lower coast forms a broad, nearly level base. The seam body's rounded lobes and
   leaf-like extension show the same limited vocabulary.
3. **Orientation and arrangement:** sizes and placements are unequal, and the two primaries face
   different directions. Arrangement is more varied than the repeated anatomy within the bodies.
4. **Water relationships:** a broad central ocean is readable. The central body's deep narrow
   recesses terminate against angular shoulders; these cuts dominate the local margin character.
5. **Margin islands:** islands sit near identifiable margins, with unequal sizes. Several still
   make short bead/sliver sequences, particularly beside the rounded subordinates; this is a
   concern rather than the decisive failure for this sparse row.
6. **Half scale:** the two-primary hierarchy survives, but so do the flat base, side paddle and
   bent appendage. Smaller islands disappear without improving the macro outline.

Decisive failures: **R3**, with **R2** from the repeated body/appendage vocabulary. The actionable
problem is the geometry of the main coast transitions, not a shortage of small coastal detail.
