Counts describe what is visible after connecting seam pieces; they are not owner
labels substituted for visual hierarchy. All ordinary rows show one to three
primary masses. Axes, facing margins and water openings vary, with generally
coherent ocean negative space. No decisive R1 ribbon or R6 cosmetic rescue is
observed. Island sizes and locations generally relate to specific margins;
localized R5 concerns remain as stated. Extra resolution does not cure either
failing row.

Compared with issue 183, the B repair is material and several formerly failing
rows improve. A and Large's retired upper shoulders also change the shape, but
the extended opposing banks can still project as nearly straight shelves. This
is a geometry/curvature problem, not an argument to change seeds, projection,
coverage tolerances or rendering. Uniform quota refitting and resulting guard/site
changes legitimately alter placement under the unchanged deterministic algorithm;
this is not a matched-pixel comparison with old placements held fixed.

Two material states and one matrix are spent. Any last state must explicitly
address these remaining banks while preserving the fixed role geometry and
all targets, then rerun the complete readiness and world gates. It cannot reuse
a numeric pass as visual acceptance. The separate independent review will retain
its own row judgments; this main-task review does not select a family or ADR.
