# Issue 184: curved bay transitions — not recommended

The final local recipe passes all 140 body-only certificates and all 134 paid
construction-and-placement inputs, with exact repeats. The second and final
nine-row world comparison passes every numerical gate, but the main task's
[review of all eighteen images](visual-review-r2.md) rejects the family. A's
long opposing lips in normal-01 remain decisive, and repeated B/C/control mouths
remain visible. Large's formerly straight upper shelf is materially improved,
especially in default-004; that improvement does not rescue the family.
The [independent final review](independent-world-review-r2.md) agrees: normal-01,
normal-02, normal-03 and both controls fail R3; normal-04 and all three recovered
large-primary rows provisionally pass with concerns. The family is not selected for
production, and no human acceptance is established.

This investigation replaces specified A, B and large-primary bay intervals with
four sampled cubic pieces. The sampled polygon remains authoritative. It keeps
raw roles, their roots and collars, interior witnesses, paid quotas, seed scopes,
finite budgets and the existing certificate and placement rules. C and
subordinate geometry remain unchanged. The revised large branch is explicitly
versioned; it is not represented as unchanged issue-182 geometry.

The [final hypothesis](revision-r2.md) distributes turning along A's lower bank
and bows Large's upper bank. It does not assume that unchanged B, C or control
concerns are resolved. All three material states and both world comparisons are
spent. No fourth state or third matrix is available under this issue. Any further
constructor must have separate scope and reconsider how bays relate to the broad
body; another local curve adjustment is outside this completed experiment.

The final read-only world check passes with 50 current captured sources, 88
relative imports, eighteen verified images, 134 condensed gate entries and the
first comparison's manifest/results bound as predecessor evidence. Numerical and
provenance success does not override the visual rejection.
